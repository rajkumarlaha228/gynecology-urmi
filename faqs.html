Robotic Gynaecological Surgery

Robotic gynaecological surgery is an advanced minimally invasive surgical technique that uses robotic-assisted technology to help surgeons perform complex procedures with enhanced visualisation, precision and control. It may be considered for selected gynaecological conditions based on individual medical requirements.

Robotic surgery may be considered for selected cases involving uterine fibroids, endometriosis, ovarian conditions and other complex gynaecological problems. The appropriate surgical approach depends on the diagnosis, medical history and overall health of the patient.

For suitable patients, robotic-assisted surgery may offer enhanced visualisation, precise instrument control and smaller surgical incisions. Recovery experiences vary depending on the procedure, individual health and surgical circumstances.

Advanced Laparoscopic Surgery

Advanced laparoscopic surgery is a minimally invasive surgical approach performed through small incisions using a camera and specialised instruments. It allows surgeons to examine and treat various gynaecological conditions without the need for a large abdominal incision in suitable cases.

Laparoscopic procedures may be used to manage uterine fibroids, ovarian cysts, endometriosis, pelvic adhesions and other gynaecological conditions. The suitability of this approach is determined after a detailed clinical evaluation.

Laparoscopic surgery uses small incisions, a camera and specialised instruments, whereas open surgery generally involves a larger incision. The choice depends on the patient's condition, surgical complexity and medical assessment.

High-Risk Pregnancy & Complex Obstetric Care

High-risk pregnancy management involves specialised medical supervision for pregnancies where certain maternal or foetal conditions may increase the risk of complications. It includes regular consultations, appropriate investigations and careful monitoring to support the health of both mother and baby.

Conditions such as gestational diabetes, high blood pressure, pre-eclampsia, multiple pregnancies, thyroid disorders and previous pregnancy complications may require specialised monitoring and individualised care.

Complex obstetric care involves managing pregnancies with challenging medical or pregnancy-related complications. It may include specialised maternal assessments, foetal monitoring, treatment planning and coordination of care throughout pregnancy and delivery.

The frequency of consultations depends on the pregnancy condition, maternal health and baby's development. Your obstetrician will recommend an appropriate monitoring schedule based on your individual medical needs.

Fibroid, Ovarian & Endometriosis Care

Uterine fibroids are non-cancerous growths that develop in or around the uterus. They may cause heavy menstrual bleeding, pelvic pressure, abdominal discomfort, frequent urination or fertility-related concerns. Treatment depends on their size, location and symptoms.

Fibroid surgery may be recommended when growths cause significant symptoms, affect fertility or create other medical concerns. Depending on individual circumstances, treatment may involve myomectomy or another suitable surgical approach.

Ovarian surgery involves treating certain ovarian conditions, such as persistent or complex cysts, ovarian masses and other abnormalities. Depending on the findings, the procedure may involve removing a cyst while preserving ovarian tissue whenever clinically appropriate.

Endometriosis is a condition in which tissue similar to the lining of the uterus grows outside the uterus. Common symptoms include painful periods, chronic pelvic pain, pain during intercourse and sometimes difficulty conceiving.

Endometriosis management depends on the severity of symptoms, extent of the condition and fertility goals. Treatment may include medication, hormonal management, pain relief or minimally invasive surgery in selected cases.

Infertility-Related Care

Infertility refers to difficulty achieving pregnancy after a period of regular unprotected intercourse. Couples are generally advised to seek evaluation after 12 months of trying, or after 6 months when the woman is aged 35 or older. Earlier assessment may be appropriate in the presence of known reproductive health concerns.

Infertility evaluation may include menstrual and ovulation assessment, hormonal blood tests, pelvic ultrasound and other investigations. Male fertility assessment, including semen analysis, may also be recommended to understand the possible causes.
